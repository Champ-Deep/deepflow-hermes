# Brand foundation

Two lists. The first is fixed and Hermes enforces it. The second is Sreekar's to decide, and until he decides, Hermes uses the recommended default and labels it as a default.

## Fixed (do not change without Sreekar and Champ both saying yes)

**Name.** Deep Flow. Two words in prose, "deepflow" or "DEEPFLOW" only as a wordmark or handle.

**Promise.** Flow state of mind to the grind state of life.

**What it is.** A gathering point for people who practice movement and flow arts, anchored by a small stable near Bangalore. Not a wellness brand, not a gym, not a yoga studio, not a productivity hack.

**Manifesto.** Flow states change lives. The best art happens when you combine technology and craft. Events should leave people feeling connected to people who get it. The community is a movement, not a membership.

**Voice.** Wry but warm. Confident because the thing has been done, not because expertise is being performed. Specific to Bangalore and to this stable; nothing that could be pasted onto another brand.

**Allowed vocabulary.** Flow, presence, state, motion, grounded, edge, build, ride, craft, earned. Wellness words are allowed only when tied to a physical experience ("the stillness after a session", not "unlocking your potential").

**Banned.**
- CTAs: "Submit", "Register Now", "Sign up". Use "Come in", "Reserve your spot", "Save me a place", "Ride with us".
- Words: wellness journey, transformative, unlock, empower, elevate, holistic (as a label), mindful (as a label), tribe, vibe (in copy), community (as a buzzword; say who and what instead), and everything on the no-ai-slop banned list.
- Cliches in visuals: mandalas, gradient sunsets, lotus, sans serif on beige minimalism, stock photos of people meditating, 3D blobs, purple to blue gradients.
- Emoji: one per post maximum, only when it adds meaning. None in headings. None in email.
- Em dashes and en dashes: none, anywhere, including headings, titles, table cells, date ranges and alt text. Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or restructure.

**Audience.** Consumers, not businesses. Adults in Bangalore who spin poi, hoop, staff, fans, or want to start; riders and people who want to be around horses; the people who come to a showcase or a fire night because a friend said "you have to see this". Corporate groups are a secondary audience, served through Champions Ranch, not through Deep Flow's own voice.

**Photography.** Golden hour, bodies in motion or just after, dust in the light, a poi trail mid arc, hands on a horse's neck, embers. Unstaged, never stock, film like warmth, asymmetric framing. Text over a photo only with a solid band or a 60 to 70 percent overlay.

**Writing gate.** Every piece of human facing writing passes the `no-ai-slop` skill before it goes out. Every page passes the `consumer-landing-page` quality gate.

## Three tests for anything brand facing

1. **The swap test.** Could another stable, studio or community put their name on it unchanged? If yes, it is not Deep Flow. Make it specific or cut it.
2. **The read aloud test.** Would Sreekar say this sentence to a friend at the stable? If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
3. **The golden hour test.** Does the visual feel like 6 pm light on open ground, or like a stock library? If stock, replace or drop.
