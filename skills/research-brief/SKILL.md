# Research brief

A brief answers the question in the first two lines, shows the evidence under it, and ends with the next action. Sreekar reads a third of anything; the top carries it.

## When to use

Partner and venue scouting, prop suppliers, pricing benchmarks for workshops and sessions in Bangalore, what other flow arts groups and stables do, events in the next two weeks, who runs a page or org, and the daily community check.

## Procedure

1. Restate the question in one line and name the decision it feeds. If there is no decision, ask what the answer is for.
2. Search wide for ten minutes, not sixty: official sites, Instagram pages (public), event platforms, maps, one or two forums. Note the source for every fact.
3. Prefer primary sources and dated pages. Mark anything older than a year as "old". Mark anything you could not verify as "unverified".
4. Write the brief:
   - Lines 1 and 2: the answer and the recommended next action.
   - A short table: option, why it fits, verdict (approach first, shortlist, reference), next step. Five rows or fewer.
   - Evidence: one line per source with the link.
   - Open questions: what only a call or a visit will settle.
5. Save to `proposals/research-<topic>-<date>.md`. Send the first two lines plus the table in chat.

## Scene monitoring (the community check job)

Check, in this order, and only report what is new since the last run (keep seen items in `~/.hermes/seen-events.json`): Deep Flow's own Instagram mentions and comments (read only); IndieFlow's events; Bangalore flow, juggling, circus, acro, ecstatic dance and drum circle pages; horse and ranch events near Sarjapur; city listings (Insider, BookMyShow, AllEvents) filtered to arts and outdoors in the next 14 days. For each new item: one line, a link, a suggested action (attend, share, partner, ignore). No posting, no commenting, no DMs.

## Pricing benchmarks

When asked what to charge: collect five real prices for comparable sessions in Bangalore with the link, note what each includes, and give a range with Sreekar's posture from `context/02` applied. Never present a single number as "the market rate".

## Pitfalls

- A list of links with no answer at the top.
- Numbers without a source, or a single source presented as consensus.
- Research as a way to delay a decision that has enough evidence already. Say "enough to decide" when it is.
- Reading private groups or scraping personal data. Public pages only.

## Verification

First two lines answer and recommend; every fact has a source; the file is saved; the open questions are real ones.
